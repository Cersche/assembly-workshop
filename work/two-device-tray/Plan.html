Two devices on one tray

Brian: “we should be able to mount two devices on a tray, space permitting.” Plan only — nothing built yet. Fit, retention and strength stay UNKNOWN.

1. How it's saved

  • A slot keeps its first device exactly as today: equipment + tray. Old slots and old revisions load and build unchanged.
  • A second device is one optional extra entry on the slot: second: {equipment: "eq-…", tray: {…}}. Only one extra device (two total).
  • Shared by the whole slot (stay in the first tray): rack units, tray–faceplate joint, keystones, rear-post support and bracket style.
  • Each device has its own: left/right position, front gap, rotation, how it attaches (screws, pegs, clips, strap, flat), standoff, front position/bezel. A sleeve can't share a tray in v1 (it boxes the whole front in).
  • The SCAD template gets a second device block (dev2_*) that is off unless a second device is set, so a one-device tray produces the same file as today.

2. “Space permitting” — what's checked

  • Both footprints together fit inside the 204 mm tray limit (208 rack opening minus 2 mm each side), with the 8 mm border on the outside.
  • The devices don't overlap and keep at least a 4 mm gap between them (proposed; you can tell me a different number). Strap slots and foot pockets of one device can't cut into the other.
  • Both devices' front openings fit on the faceplate with 2 mm webs between every opening, and stay clear of the rack-hole slots, keystones and tray screw joints.
  • The taller device sets the needed rack units; the tray depth follows the deeper one.
  • When it doesn't fit, you get a plain sentence (e.g. “Together they're 221 mm wide; the tray allows 204. Try rotating one.”) and Apply/export stay blocked, same as today.

3. Ports and openings

  • Each device's front openings follow that device, as now. Rear ports go into the cable planner as separate ports labelled with the device name, so cables can connect to either.
  • Keystones stay one shared list for the faceplate.
  • The rack view shows both device previews on the tray; the clearance review screens the combined envelope.

4. Place on tray window — pick one

A · Device tabs

One tab per device plus a “Slot” tab for shared things (U, joint, keystones, rear support). “+ Add device” becomes a second tab; ✕ on a tab removes it. Drag either device in the sketch; clicking one switches tabs.

+ Today's form stays almost the same; least clutter.

− You only see one device's settings at a time.

B · Two columns

Sketch on top; both devices' settings side by side underneath, shared slot settings below them. Empty right column shows “+ Add a second device”.

+ Compare both at a glance.

− Window gets wide/long; cramped on the laptop.

C · Sketch-first list

Big sketch; a small device list beside it (like layers). Select in the list or sketch, then the one settings panel below edits that device. Slot settings stay in their own section.

+ Feels like arranging things on a bench.

− Biggest change to the current window.

My pick: A. It changes the least, keeps the window small, and merges cleanly with the keystone-picker work happening in the same file.
