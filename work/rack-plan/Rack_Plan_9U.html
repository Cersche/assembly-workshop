4. Cooling (right side view, wall-mounted)

  • Fan 1 (must-have): 80 mm 5 V PWM on the right side panel, rear, U1–U2. Pulls the OptiPlex's exhaust out of the ~34 mm pocket behind it before it hits the wall, and cools the HDD.
  • Tray-side opening: slots or a window on the right side of the OptiPlex tray behind the PC (Y ~195–228 mm), lined up with Fan 1. The faceplate, orientation and sleeve mounting stay as settled.
  • Fan 2 (optional): same fan, high on the right side at U8–U9, for the modem, switch and Pi.
  • Intake (optional): filtered, at the bottom front, never under the rear floor opening, where it would push against the PC's exhaust.
  • Side panels: vented on the right, mostly solid on the left (sleeve side). Keep 3–5 cm behind the rack if the wall mount allows.
  • Control: DS18B20 sensors in the OptiPlex pocket and up high, fans on Pi GPIO PWM, Home Assistant curve (off below ~30 °C, full at ~45 °C), shown on the dashboard. Fans run off their own 5 V supply.
  • Check: HDD temp via smartctl, Pi via vcgencmd. Target HDD under ~45 °C.

6. Shopping and to-do

  • TP-Link TL-SG108E (have it; all 8 ports used)
  • 1–2× 80 mm 5 V PWM fans (e.g. Noctua NF-A8 5V PWM), 5 V supply, 2× DS18B20 sensors, dust filter if adding an intake
  • Photo of the OptiPlex 7010's back and right side vents, so the openings line up
  • Hosyond 7" 1024×600 HDMI touch screen (~$46). Measure it on arrival: outside size, visible area, ear hole positions, which micro-USB port is touch.
  • Right-angle micro-HDMI to HDMI cable, ~30 cm
  • Down-angled USB-A to micro-USB cable, ~30 cm
  • 4× 15 cm + 2× 20 cm slim Ethernet patch cables, one color
  • 3× blank keystone inserts
  • HDD 12 V power adapter, if the USB-SATA adapter doesn't include one
  • Replacement 2.5" SSD, later. Leave a cable loop by the empty SSD pad.
  • Faceplates to design later: switch (U8) and keystone (U7) lined up, Pi + Hue tray (U6) with the USB slot, 3U screen plate (U5–U3). Needs the switch model and screen measurements.

*Not confirmed yet: the HDD connection (plan assumes USB), switch model and port spacing, screen dimensions. Physical fit, cable lengths and screen mounting strength are untested. If the Pi struggles to power the screen (low-voltage warning, flicker), move the screen to its own power adapter.
