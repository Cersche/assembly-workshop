What already exists

  • The rack viewport renders the real source meshes with shadows, ambient occlusion and per-triangle picking (rack-studio.js). Picking currently resolves to a slot, not to anything smaller.
  • Slot equipment in the rack view is the shared studio tray and plate shape, not each device's own mesh, so nothing on it is individually addressable yet.
  • Port data is thin. Wizard equipment stores front faceplate cutouts (label, x, z, w, h) and keystone X positions. Nothing records rear ports or connector types, and Pi 4 ports are only cutouts in pi4_baseline.scad.
  • The camera offers Front and Perspective. There is no rear view, and the top fan panel and side panels always draw when enabled.

The cable guide you supplied

Lab Rax Cable Guide by mklements (Bambu Studio 3MF, 708 triangles, 28 × 28 × 44 mm). It takes 1U on a vertical post and fixes with two M6 × 10 mm screws into the post's existing holes. Its own description says it is meant for the rear posts. It becomes the default guide: placed on RL, RR, FL or FR at any U, snapped to that U's hole pair.

Its license is MakerWorld Exclusive, which is stricter than the creator parts' BY-NC-SA. I propose treating it like them: a local preview mesh only, never redistributed, and kept out of PRINT THE RACK unless you say otherwise. I left the file in Downloads and copied nothing into the project.

Unrestricted views

  • Front, Rear and Perspective presets, plus orbit all the way round.
  • A Cables view mode that hides the side panels, top fan panel and bottom panel and ghosts the frame at low opacity, so cables and ports read clearly from either side.
  • Optional depth cut: a slider that slices the rack front-to-back to see a tray's interior.

Everything selectable

  • Each port, keystone (front and rear face), cable guide, cable and entry/exit point is its own pick target with a stable ID, for example slot2/rear/dc.
  • Click selects; hover shows a chip like the one in the diagram. The inspector gets a Port, Cable and Guide panel; right-click and the ellipsis menu carry Connect, Route, Remove.
  • Selecting a cable lights its whole run, both ends and any front patch it continues through.

Rendered nicely

  • Cables as smooth tubes with slight sag between guides, bundled side by side through each guide instead of overlapping.
  • Connector-shaped port markers (RJ45, USB-C, HDMI, barrel, IEC) with a soft glow on hover and the lime selection outline used elsewhere.
  • Type colors you can override per cable; the existing shadow and AO passes apply to cables too.

In and out of the cabinet

  • Exit points placed at bottom-rear, top-rear or either side, drawn as a grommet marker.
  • Outside endpoints with a name and kind: wall outlet, UPS, modem, wall jack, desk, another rack.
  • A cable may end at an outside endpoint and must then pass through an exit point; the diagram draws it as an arrow off the frame.

Routing suggestions

A suggestions list in the Review tab, each one with a Show me that highlights the cables involved and, where it is safe, an Apply that only changes the unsaved draft:

  • Keep power on one rear post and data on the other; flag cables that cross between them.
  • Add a guide where a vertical run goes more than 2U without one, at the U it suggests.
  • Send cables that leave the cabinet through one exit point, and keep the top fan panel clear.
  • Use a keystone pass-through for connections you unplug often, so the rear run stays put.
  • Leave a short service loop at each device; runs under 1U need no guide.
  • Flag tight turns using generic published bend-radius guidance (for example 4 × cable diameter for Cat6), shown as guidance, not as a check of your cables.
  • Warn when a guide is crowded past a set number of cables. The guide's real capacity is UNKNOWN, so this threshold is a setting.
  • Find ports with nothing plugged in and cables with only one end.

The wiring diagram output

  • Wiring sheet: front and rear elevations side by side, cable numbers, guides, exit points and outside endpoints, in the style above, exported as SVG and as a single printable page.
  • Cable schedule: the table above as CSV, sorted by cable number.
  • Labels: optional sheet of wrap labels, one per cable end (C7 · K1 → Modem).
  • Generated from saved data only, like the other exports. It never saves the draft and never touches STL output.

Data model

A new cabling block in the rack document, saved through the existing atomic Save and kept in rack history:

  • ports per equipment item: id, label, connector type, face (front or rear), position. Wizard equipment gets a Ports step that adds type and face to today's cutouts; Pi 4 gets a fixed preset. Keystones become pass-through ports with a front and a rear face.
  • guides: id, kind (default Lab Rax Cable Guide), post, U.
  • exits and endpoints: placed openings and named outside devices.
  • cables: id, number, type, color, from, to, route (ordered guide and exit IDs), service loop, note.

This adds no hardware pins, no geometry and no STL changes. Racks saved without a cabling block load unchanged.

Suggested order

Step 1

Views and guides

Rear preset and Cables view mode; load the guide mesh locally; place, move and remove guides on posts.

Step 2

Ports

Port data model, Pi 4 preset, wizard Ports step, keystone pass-through; selectable port markers and the Port panel.

Step 3

Cables, exits and endpoints

Connect mode (port to port, or port to outside endpoint), automatic routing through the nearest guides, tube rendering.

Step 4

Suggestions

The rule list above in Review, with Show me and Apply to draft.

Step 5

Wiring sheet

SVG and printable sheet, CSV schedule, labels.

Limits that stay visible

  • Cable guide fit, strength, retention and capacity: UNKNOWN. M6 hole compatibility is the creator's statement, not checked here.
  • Cable lengths are planning estimates from the route plus slack; real cable OD, stiffness and connector depth are UNKNOWN, so rear clearance behind equipment is not checked.
  • Port positions for wizard equipment are what you enter; Pi 4 rear-port positions depend on tray orientation and need confirming against the Pi reference.
  • No printing, purchases or physical fitting is part of this.
