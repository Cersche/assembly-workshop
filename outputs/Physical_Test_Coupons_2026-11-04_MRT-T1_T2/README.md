# MRT-T1 and MRT-T2: MoCA tray channel test prints (2026-11-04)

PROPOSED, UNPRINTED. Fit, retention and strength are UNKNOWN. Built from `src/MRT-E.scad`; source of the tray and plate these test.

Load `MRT-T1_T2_channel_test_prints.3mf` (two objects), or the two STLs.

## MRT-T1 channel forming jig
218 x 203 footprint, 22 tall, about 82 cm3 (the tray and plate are 257 cm3).
Every channel, lip and the rear rib, plus the ScreenBeam foot pockets and the splitter riser with its M3 insert bores, in the tray's exact positions and at the tray's heights above the floor. The floor is thin and open (2 mm instead of 4).

Use: set the real ScreenBeam and splitter on it, form the coax in the channels, and cut and crimp the jumpers to what you see. Weigh or hold the jig down: it is lighter and floppier than the tray, so it won't show how the finished tray takes the cable's push.

## MRT-T2 notch slice
38 x 34 footprint, 22 tall, about 8 cm3.
Three short channels with the tray's wall lip and rib lip, at lip gaps 6.4, 6.8 (the tray's) and 7.4, numbered on the floor.

Use: push the real coax in to see which gap holds it without a fight.

## Not tested by these
The M6 plate joint, the shoes, the rack fit and the Velcro slots.
Assumed until real gear is on the jig: F connector length 26, coax 7 OD, LAN and power plug lengths.

## Report back
Which gap holds best, whether any bend fights the coax, and any connector or port that lands off the channel.
